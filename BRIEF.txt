Act as a senior product designer, UX writer, and frontend engineer.

I am building the first landing page for an early-stage Nigerian commerce project called Hayzed.

This is NOT yet a full e-commerce platform.

The purpose of this version is to:
1. Clearly communicate value to potential customers.
2. Collect interested buyers' contact details.
3. Learn what products and shopping problems customers actually have.
4. Give us a professional web presence while we continue researching merchants.
5. Give customers a way to join our early-access list through email and WhatsApp.
6. Allow us to improve the website gradually as we learn more.

IMPORTANT PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY

Design the page CUSTOMER-FIRST.

Do not make the page primarily about:
- Hayzed
- our technology
- our marketplace vision
- AI
- merchant digitization
- how innovative we are
- features we have not built

A customer does not care that we are building a marketplace.

The page should first answer:

"What value does this give me?"

CURRENT CUSTOMER PROBLEM WE ARE EXPLORING

In many parts of Lagos/Nigeria, people know that products they need may exist in nearby physical markets and shops, but finding them can be inefficient.

The current journey can look like:

Need a product
→ ask people where it might be sold
→ travel to a market
→ walk from shop to shop
→ compare products/prices
→ negotiate
→ arrange transport/delivery
→ sometimes leave without finding exactly what was needed

At the same time, many good local merchants have large physical inventories that are difficult for customers to discover online.

Our hypothesis is that customers may value being able to discover products from trusted local merchants before travelling around looking for them.

Do NOT present this hypothesis as something we have already proven.

CURRENT VALUE PROPOSITION

Use a customer-facing idea around:

"Find what you need from trusted local sellers, without searching shop by shop."

Alternative wording can be explored, but the message should communicate:

- easier product discovery
- trusted local sellers
- knowing what is available before travelling
- convenient communication with sellers
- potential local pickup/delivery
- saving time and effort

Do NOT promise:
- same-day delivery
- guaranteed lowest prices
- verified sellers unless we actually implement verification
- thousands of products
- nationwide coverage
- secure payments
- installment payments
- anything we cannot currently deliver

Hayzed is currently in an early-access/research stage.

Primary initial audience:

People in Lagos who regularly buy:
- home appliances
- electronics
- household items
- fashion/accessories
- everyday products from physical merchants

They may currently find sellers through:
- WhatsApp
- referrals
- Jiji

- Instagram
- physical markets

The first version may initially focus on products from merchants we personally know and research.

BRAND

Brand name: Hayzed

Domain will likely be:
hayzed.com.ng

The brand should feel:
- trustworthy
- local
- modern
- straightforward
- useful
- human
- not overly corporate
- not like a Silicon Valley startup template

PAGE STRUCTURE

Build a responsive single-page landing page.

1. NAVIGATION

Simple navigation.

Logo/wordmark:
Hayzed

Possible links:
How it works
For buyers
For merchants
Join early access

Primary CTA:
Join Early Access

Keep navigation minimal.

2. HERO SECTION

The hero must lead with CUSTOMER VALUE.

Do not write:
"We are building the future of Nigerian commerce."

Instead use something closer to:

"Find what you need without searching shop by shop."

Supporting copy example:

"Discover products from trusted local sellers and know what's available before making the trip."

Another possible variation:

"Find products from local sellers before you spend hours searching the market."

CTA:
Join Early Access

Secondary CTA:
I'm a Merchant

Include a small reassurance:

"Hayzed is currently launching with selected local merchants."

The hero should immediately explain what Hayzed does without requiring someone to understand marketplaces or technology.

3. CUSTOMER PROBLEM SECTION

Use something like:

"Shopping locally shouldn't mean searching blindly."

Show a simple customer journey.

Today:

Need something
→ travel around
→ ask multiple sellers
→ compare manually
→ arrange transport

With Hayzed:

Search
→ discover nearby options
→ talk to seller
→ choose how to receive it

Do not exaggerate capabilities that are not yet implemented.

This section should help visitors immediately recognize the problem.

4. VALUE / BENEFITS SECTION

Focus on outcomes, not features.

Possible benefits:

Know what's available
See products before travelling around looking for them.

Find local sellers
Discover merchants that may already have what you need nearby.

Talk before you travel
Ask questions and confirm availability first.

Choose what works for you
Pickup or delivery options can be discussed directly with sellers.

Keep copy very concise.

5. PRODUCT PREVIEW / COMING INVENTORY

We do NOT currently have a full catalogue.

Do not generate fake product listings that make it appear that customers can already buy hundreds of products.

Instead create a tasteful section saying something like:

"Products are coming."

"We're currently working directly with local merchants to bring their inventory online."

Potential categories can be displayed as placeholders:

Home Appliances
Electronics
Fashion & Accessories
Household Items

Make it visually clear that these categories are examples / coming soon.

Later this section should be easy to replace with real products.

6. EARLY ACCESS SECTION

This is one of the most important sections.

Headline:

"Tell us what you're looking for."

Instead of only saying "Join newsletter," turn the signup form into customer research.

Form fields:

Name
Email
WhatsApp number
Area / location
What are you currently looking to buy?

Optional:
"What's usually difficult when you shop for products locally?"

Checkbox / consent:
"I'd like to receive Hayzed product updates through email and WhatsApp."

The consent must not be pre-selected.

CTA:
Join Early Access

After submission show a useful confirmation message:

"You're on the list. We'll let you know as we start adding products and sellers."

Design the data structure cleanly so this can later connect to a backend/API.

For now create a clean form handler abstraction instead of hardcoding business logic into the UI.

7. FOR MERCHANTS

This should be secondary to the buyer experience.

Simple copy:

"Sell locally? We'd like to learn about your business."

"We're working directly with merchants to understand how customers discover and buy products."

CTA:
Join as a Merchant

Merchant form can collect:

Owner name
Business name
Phone/WhatsApp
Email
Business category
Market/location
How do you currently sell online?
Biggest challenge finding customers

Keep this secondary.

8. TRUST / EARLY STAGE TRANSPARENCY

Include a small section explaining:

"Hayzed is starting small."

"We're currently working directly with local merchants and customers in Lagos to understand how local buying and selling can work better online."

This transparency is intentional.

We don't want to pretend we are already a huge marketplace.

9. WHATSAPP CTA

WhatsApp will be an important communication channel.

Include a floating or visible WhatsApp contact button.

But do not make it aggressive or intrusive.

Possible CTA:
"Chat with Hayzed"

Make the WhatsApp phone number configurable through environment/configuration rather than embedding it everywhere.

10. FOOTER

Hayzed

Short description:
"Helping people discover products from local sellers."

Include:
Email
WhatsApp
Privacy
Terms

Placeholder email:
hello@hayzed.com.ng

Do not invent physical addresses or company-registration claims.

DESIGN DIRECTION

Mobile-first.

A large percentage of users will likely access this through WhatsApp links on mobile devices.

Prioritize:
- fast loading
- large readable typography
- excellent mobile layout
- obvious CTAs
- simple navigation
- high contrast
- generous spacing
- minimal JavaScript
- accessibility
- SEO basics
- semantic HTML

The design should feel warm and commerce-oriented rather than technical.

Avoid a dashboard-looking interface.

Use photography/product imagery carefully.

If real merchant/product photos are not currently available, use neutral visual placeholders rather than misleading fake inventory.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

First inspect the existing repository and determine its framework and conventions.

Do not unnecessarily replace the existing stack.

If this is an empty/new project and a choice must be made, use:

Next.js
TypeScript
Tailwind CSS

Use reusable components, but do not over-engineer.

Suggested components:

Navbar
Hero
ProblemJourney
Benefits
CategoryPreview
EarlyAccessForm
MerchantSection
TrustSection
WhatsAppCTA
Footer

Keep content easy to edit because our messaging will change as customer research progresses.

Create a central content/config file where possible rather than scattering copy throughout components.

Create clear types/interfaces for forms.

Forms should:
- validate fields
- have useful error messages
- support loading state
- support successful submission state
- work well on mobile
- be accessible

Do not create a fake backend.

If there is currently no backend/API:
abstract submission behind a function/service and clearly mark where the future API integration goes.

Analytics readiness:

Prepare meaningful event names for future analytics, such as:

early_access_started
early_access_submitted
merchant_interest_started
merchant_interest_submitted
whatsapp_clicked

Do not install unnecessary analytics services yet unless one already exists.

Include sensible Open Graph metadata.

choose the customer outcome.

Bad:

Better:
"Know what's available before you spend time searching."

Bad:
"Our platform connects merchants with consumers."

Better:
"Find local sellers who have what you're looking for."

Bad:
"Join our revolutionary marketplace."

Better:
"Tell us what you're looking for."

IMPORTANT PRODUCT CONSTRAINT

This is version 0.1.

Do not build:
- authentication
- shopping cart
- checkout
- payment integration
- merchant dashboard
- product management system
- order tracking
- complex backend
- admin dashboard

unless the repository already contains these and they are required for the landing page.

The goal is not to simulate a finished marketplace.

The goal is:

customer value proposition
+
interest collection
+
customer research
+
merchant interest
+
WhatsApp/email acquisition

Before coding:

1. Inspect the repository.
2. Briefly explain the proposed page hierarchy.
3. Identify anything in the existing project that should be reused.
4. Then implement the landing page.
5. Run the available lint/typecheck/build checks.
6. Fix issues you introduce.
7. Summarize what was implemented and where the main copy/configuration can be changed.

Most importantly:

Treat this as the beginning of a company learning about its customers, not the launch of a completed e-commerce product.
